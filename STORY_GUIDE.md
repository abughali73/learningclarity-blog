# Story guide for blog posts

Lessons from the owner's five stories (Clara the teacher, Joseph the new sales manager, Sarah the call-center agent,
Nevine the recruiter, Tarek the plant accountant), for every story post. The publishing agent reads this with AGENT.md.
Story ideas live in `_data/story_library.yml`.

## What makes these stories work

**1. The hook is a moment, not a premise.** Each one opens on a time, a place and a jolt:
"It was 4:30 PM on a Tuesday when the emergency email hit everyone's inbox", a whiteboard covered in red marker,
a contest banner flashing over a loud call-center floor, the hum of processing belts on a heavy Thursday.
Open with one concrete sensory detail and an event that changes the character's week. Never open with the product.

**2. Someone else sets the stakes.** A manager, a director, a client, a contest: another person gives a task, a deadline
and a consequence ("by next Monday", "the board meets next Tuesday", "heavy contract penalties"). External stakes make
the reader care before any feature appears. Put the deadline in the first 100 words.

**3. The gap is named honestly.** The character is good at their job but faces something new (peer to boss, routine
accounting to board strategy, reactive to high-volume hiring). The stories list the three or four specific skills
they lack. Specific, professional gaps make the reader think "that's me".

**4. The character rejects the noise.** "YouTube tips and thick books wouldn't give him a structure by Monday."
One sentence on why the usual options fail sets up a single, clear choice.

**5. One sentence starts the change.** The character types their real situation as a topic description. Quote it.
Readers learn the most useful habit (describe your situation precisely) without being told.

**6. A day-by-day montage maps features to real life.** Each day pairs one feature with one real-life slot:
Simplify late at night, Listen on a lunch break or a factory walk-through, a podcast on the commute, Visualize when
ideas blur, Quiz Mode with "I don't know" when confidence outruns knowledge, Talk to This to rehearse out loud.
The feature is always something the character does, at a believable time.

**7. There is a setback.** The quiz that stings, the "I don't know" that exposes a weak spot. Tension is what makes
the payoff feel earned. Every story needs one honest wobble.

**8. Proof before the payoff.** The exam and the verifiable certificate give the character (and the reader) evidence,
not just a feeling. The verification link sent to the boss is a satisfying, concrete beat.

**9. The payoff is a human scene.** The pipeline review, the first contest call, the compound gate on Monday, the
board's hardest question. A short quote from someone who matters (GM, CFO, client) closes the arc.

**10. Takeaways and a call to action.** Three short, transferable lessons, then one concrete next step for the reader.

Tone: calm, confident and professional; short paragraphs; plain words; light cinematic detail; the character is
competent, never hopeless. Emotional arc: pressure and self-doubt, then focus, a wobble, readiness, and quiet pride.

## What to improve on (and the rules that keep stories honest)

- **Vary the formula.** Not every story is "six days, a Monday payoff and a 30-question exam". Use different time
  spans (an evening, three weeks, a term), different payoffs (understanding a child's homework, a conversation in a
  new language, a passed school exam) and different storytelling approaches (see below). Never reuse the same
  structure in two consecutive posts.
- **Not every story ends with a certificate.** Use certificates only when proof matters to someone in the story.
- **Product claims come only from `_data/capabilities.yml`.** Use the exact feature names (Simplify, Explain
  Further, Listen, Visualize, Quiz Mode, Exam Mode, Talk to This). The product never "designs a plan for your
  deadline", never builds the character's spreadsheet, funnel or strategy, never role-plays a customer, and never
  scores a voice. The character does the real-world work; LearningClarity helps them learn.
- **Results must be realistic and attributable.** What the product can plausibly give: understanding, practice,
  a clear picture of weak areas, confidence from rehearsal, a passed exam and a verifiable certificate. Business
  results (a won contest, a percentage saved, a hire filled) belong to the character's own work, and the story says so.
- **Label fiction.** Say once in the body that the people and companies are fictional (and in `ad.footnote`).
  Quotes inside the story are the characters' words, never presented as real customer testimonials. Never invent
  statistics about LearningClarity or its users.
- **Certificates:** named after the topic (never "official" or a made-up title); not accredited; anyone can verify
  them without an account. A certificate needs an exam with 70% or more, at least 30 questions, covering every subtopic.
- **Talk to This sessions are short.** Describe several short calls, not an hour-long session.
- **Avoid sensitive territory.** No medical, legal or financial advice presented as fact, no real people or brands,
  no claims that the product replaces a teacher, doctor or official training.

## Storytelling approaches to rotate

1. **Countdown** (the owner's format): a deadline and a day-by-day week.
2. **Two perspectives**: alternate between two characters (a parent and a child, a teacher and a student, a manager and a new hire).
3. **Before and after**: the same scene twice, weeks apart.
4. **Diary or voice notes**: short dated entries in the character's own voice.
5. **The one hard question**: the story builds towards a single question the character finally answers.
6. **Small habit, big change**: 15 minutes a day over several weeks, with the streak as the spine.
7. **Myth versus reality**: the character's wrong belief about learning, corrected through what happens.

## Calls to action that fit

End with one concrete, low-effort step tied to the story, for example:
"Describe the one thing you need to learn by Friday, and start with the first subtopic tonight."
"Upload the notes you already have and take a five-question quiz before you reread them."
Mention the 300 free credits only where it fits naturally, and link to https://www.learningclarity.ai with descriptive text.
