It's exam week, and your course lives in a folder: a stack of lecture slides saved as PDF, a course reader and a few pages of your own notes. Instead of rereading them for the fifth time, you can **study your own PDFs with AI** and turn those files into a topic you actually work through.

## Why study from your own course material?

General study apps teach a subject in general. Your exam, though, tests *your* course: the definitions your lecturer used, the cases in your reader, the procedures in your company's handbook. When you learn from your own material, you study exactly what your course or employer requires, and nothing you'll never be asked about.

That matters for more than students. A new employee can upload an onboarding guide. A training team can start from the manual they already wrote. A parent can upload a worksheet or a set of class notes a child brought home.

## How to turn lecture slides into lessons

1. **Upload a file.** LearningClarity accepts PDFs, images (PNG, JPG or JPEG) and plain text files. Word documents and PowerPoint decks can't be uploaded directly, so export them to PDF first; every word processor and presentation tool can do this from its File menu.
2. **Let it build the topic.** LearningClarity reads the content and builds a topic from it, with its own subtopics.
3. **Choose what to focus on.** Pick the subtopics you need right now. Completion is tracked for each one, so you can see what's left before the exam.

Two things to know before you start. Video files, audio recordings and YouTube links aren't supported as sources, so stick to documents. And a PDF has to contain real text: a scanned page with no text layer, or a password-protected file, can't be read. If you only need a quick overview of a subject, typing a short description of the topic is the fastest route.

## From your notes to real understanding

Once the topic exists, it works like any other in LearningClarity. Open a subtopic in [Explain Mode, the step-by-step AI tutor](/en/2026/10/meet-your-step-by-step-ai-tutor/), which teaches one short section at a time. Select **Simplify** if a slide was too dense, or **Explain Further** for more detail and examples.

When you think you've got it, switch to **Quiz Mode** for low-pressure practice. Use the "I don't know" option instead of guessing, so your results show what you really know. Then sit an **Exam Mode** test on the whole topic, and use the AI performance summary to see which parts of your slides to revisit.

## A simple plan for exam week

- **Day 1:** upload your slides and notes, and pick the subtopics the exam covers.
- **Days 2 to 4:** work through them in Explain Mode, a few each day.
- **Day 5:** take a quiz on the weakest subtopics, then an exam on the full topic.
