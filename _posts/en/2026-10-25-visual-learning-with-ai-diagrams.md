Some ideas refuse to stick as sentences. You read about the water cycle, the parts of a cell or how a supply chain works, and the words make sense one by one, but the whole picture never forms. Visual learning with AI fixes that by turning the concept in front of you into a diagram you can actually see.

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

In LearningClarity, that is the job of **Visualize**. While you study a subtopic in Explain Mode, Visualize generates a clean, labelled diagram of the current concept. The labels are in your topic's language, so a topic you study in Spanish gets Spanish labels, and one you study in Arabic gets Arabic labels.

Here are three practical ways to use it.

## 1. Use visual learning with AI to "see" the idea first

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

When a section feels abstract, stop reading for a moment and ask for the picture. A labelled diagram shows how the parts relate: what comes first, what connects to what, and which part does which job.

A good habit is to look at the diagram, then go back to the text. The sentences suddenly have somewhere to land. If the text is still dense, **Simplify** rephrases the current section in plainer terms. If you want more, **Explain Further** adds detail and examples. You can also ask your own question about the section or ask for an example, as you would with a human tutor.

## 2. Turn diagrams into a revision tool

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

During exam week, diagrams are some of the fastest revision material you have. Try this simple routine:

1. Open the subtopic you want to revise and generate the diagram.
2. Look at it for a minute, then close it.
3. Sketch it from memory on paper, with as many labels as you can recall.
4. Compare your sketch with the original and note what you missed.

The gaps in your sketch show you exactly what to review next. Explain Mode's **Knowledge Checks**, short multiple-choice questions with feedback and **Retry**, then help you confirm you've understood before you move on.

## 3. Explain it to someone else

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

Teaching an idea is one of the best ways to learn it. A labelled diagram makes that easier, whether you are a student explaining a process to a study partner, a parent going through a homework topic with a child, or a trainer preparing a session for a team.

Because explanations and questions in LearningClarity are adapted to the learner's age, a younger learner and an adult studying the same topic each get an explanation pitched at their level, and the diagram gives both of you a shared picture to point at.

## Mix seeing, reading and listening

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

Visualize works best as one tool among several. If you'd rather hear the explanation, **Listen** reads the current section aloud, which helps when you're away from the screen.
