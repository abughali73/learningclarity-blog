Lena needed online learning with a certificate at the end, and she had six days to get it. On Friday afternoon her manager asked her to lead the new client project, with the kickoff on Monday. She nodded, smiled, and spent the train ride home wondering how to hide that she had never managed a project in her life.

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

She didn't want forty open browser tabs. She wanted to be taught properly, and to have something to show for it. This is the week of a fictional learner, one evening at a time.

## Sunday: a blank page that fills itself

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

Lena signed in to [LearningClarity, the AI learning app](https://www.learningclarity.ai) with her Microsoft account. The account opened with 300 free credits, and nobody asked for a card.

She had no textbook to upload, so she typed what was keeping her awake: "Project management for a first-time project lead: planning, scope, risks and keeping stakeholders informed." A moment later the worry had a shape: a topic with its own subtopics. She picked the ones that mattered most for Monday. (Here is more on how to [learn any subject by describing it](/en/2026/10/learn-any-subject-with-ai/).)

## Monday to Wednesday: learning in the gaps of the day

The evenings were short, so the lessons had to be too. **Explain Mode** taught one short section at a time, and Lena moved on only when she was ready. When "scope creep" sounded like jargon, **Simplify** put it in plain words. When she wanted the real thing, **Explain Further** added detail and examples, and she asked her own question: how do you decide which risk to worry about first?

On Monday night the phases of a project still blurred together. She tapped **Visualize**, and a clean, labelled diagram laid them out in order. She copied it into her notebook before bed.

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

On Tuesday she was too tired to read, so **Listen** read the section aloud while she cooked. Before bed she turned the stakeholder subtopic into an **AI podcast**, a two-voice conversation between a host and an expert guest. It was made in the background, and an "AI job complete" notification said it was ready in time for the morning train.

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

Knowledge Checks kept her honest along the way, and when she missed one, Retry gave her another go. Phone or laptop, **Resume** always put her back exactly where she had stopped.

## Thursday: the quiz that stung

Lena felt ready. She wanted to know if she was. In **Quiz Mode** she chose the subtopics and question types, and when she wasn't sure, she pressed "I don't know" instead of guessing.

Two questions on stakeholder communication caught her out, and it stung. Then she asked why the right answers were right, opened her laptop and talked it through with **Talk to This**, a live voice tutor (on the web app for now, with mobile planned). Saying the answer out loud was what made it stick.

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

## Saturday: the exam, and online learning with a certificate to show for it

{% include scene.html n=6 %}

**Exam Mode** felt formal, and that was the point. Lena set the scope to the whole topic so every subtopic was covered, chose Medium difficulty, kept it untimed and asked for 30 questions. A certificate needs a score of 70% or higher on an exam with at least 30 questions that covers every subtopic.

Her hands were cold on the trackpad. Then the results: a full question review, an AI summary of her strengths and the areas to revisit, and a score above the line. The **View Certificate** button appeared.

The certificate showed her name, the topic, the date and her result, with a credential ID and a QR code. She downloaded the PDF and sent the verification link to her manager, who opened it without signing in and saw that it was valid, what it was for, who earned it and when. Earning and verifying it cost nothing. Seven days of learning in a row had quietly earned her the **7-Day Streak** achievement too.

## Monday: the kickoff

{% include scene.html n=7 %}

Lena walked in with a diagram in her notebook, a podcast in her head and a certificate already in her manager's inbox. When the client asked how she would handle risk, she didn't bluff. She answered.

Your week can start the same way: describe the one thing you need to learn, and see where it takes you.
