Lena wanted online learning with a certificate at the end, not another folder of bookmarked articles. She had just been asked to lead her first project at work, and she wanted a solid grasp of the basics plus something she could show her manager.

This is the story of how this fictional learner went from a blank screen to a verifiable certificate in one week with LearningClarity.

## Sunday evening: sign up and describe the topic

Lena opened [LearningClarity, the AI learning app](https://www.learningclarity.ai) on her laptop and signed in with her Microsoft account. Google, Apple and email work too. Her account started with 300 free credits, and nobody asked for a card.

She didn't need a textbook to begin. She typed what she wanted to learn: "Project management fundamentals for a first-time project lead: planning, scope, risks and keeping stakeholders informed." LearningClarity built the topic, gave it a category and generated its subtopics. Lena chose the subtopics to focus on, knowing completion would be tracked for each one. (Here is more on how to [learn any subject by describing it](/en/2026/10/learn-any-subject-with-ai/).)

## Monday to Wednesday: one short section at a time

Each evening Lena opened **Explain Mode**, the step-by-step AI tutor. It taught one short section at a time, and she selected Next whenever she was ready. When the part on scope felt abstract, she tapped **Simplify** for plainer wording. On risk planning she chose **Explain Further** for more detail and examples, then asked her own question about how to rank risks.

Knowledge Checks appeared along the way: multiple-choice questions with feedback, and Retry when she missed one. On Tuesday she carried on from her phone on the train. Her library and progress were already there, and Resume took her straight back to where she had stopped.

## Thursday: an honest quiz

Before anything formal, Lena tried **Quiz Mode**. She picked the subtopics and question types, and when she wasn't sure, she chose "I don't know" instead of guessing. The results showed her score, a breakdown of correct, incorrect and "I don't know" answers, and a mastery level.

Two questions about stakeholder communication caught her out. She asked why the right answers were right, then went back to that subtopic in Explain Mode for one more pass.

## Saturday: online learning with a certificate to show for it

Now she was ready for **Exam Mode**. She set the scope to the whole topic so every subtopic was covered, chose Medium difficulty, kept it untimed and asked for 30 questions. That mattered: a certificate needs a score of 70% or higher on an exam with at least 30 questions that covers every subtopic.

Her results came with a full question review and an AI performance summary of her strengths and the areas to revisit. Her score cleared the 70% mark, and the **View Certificate** button appeared.

The certificate shows her name, the topic, the issue date and her result, with a credential ID and a QR code. Lena downloaded it as a PDF and sent the verification link to her manager, who opened it without signing in and saw that it was valid, what it was awarded for, who received it and when. Earning and verifying certificates costs nothing.

On Monday, Lena walked into the kickoff knowing the material, with the proof already in her manager's inbox.

Your own week can start the same way: describe the first topic you want to learn, and see where it takes you.
