**Monday, 21:40.** The message from Dr. Haddad arrived while Alicia was still wearing her lab coat: "Embryology block exam Friday at nine. Vivas in the afternoon. Be ready to explain week one, from zygote to implantation, out loud." Four days. These are the diary entries she kept. Alicia, her lecturer and her university are fictional; the embryology is real course content.

## Monday night: embryology study notes, one section at a time

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

"Rereading the textbook won't get me to Friday. I need embryology study notes I actually understand, not more highlighted pages.

I opened [the LearningClarity study platform](https://www.learningclarity.ai) and typed my course: 'Human Embryology and Fetal Development for second-year medical students.' It built the topic and its subtopics. I picked one: Cleavage, Blastocyst Formation, and Implantation.

Explain Mode teaches it one short section at a time, and each section opens with a short clinical scenario. Cleavage first: the zygote divides into two, four, eight and more blastomeres, each smaller than the last, all still inside the zona pellucida. I pressed Visualize."

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

"'Help me remember' gave me: *One, two, many, wrapped.* Then 'Show me a video' suggested YouTube explanations, one on fertilization, cleavage and blastulation, and a Khan Academy video on early embryogenesis. I watched two before bed."

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

## Tuesday: morula, blastocyst and a name tag

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

"**07:15, bus.** I pressed Listen and let it read the morula section aloud: a ball of about 16 cells, and compaction, when the cells press tightly together. Memory hook: *Mulberry mound.*

**Evening.** Fluid seeps in, a cavity forms, and the cells separate into an outer trophoblast and an inner embryoblast. Then the blastocyst hatches from the zona pellucida. *Blast in a cyst with a cell nest.* A Common mistake callout stopped me: the trophoblast is not the embryo itself."

"Implantation lost me at apposition, adhesion and invasion, so I tapped Explain simpler, then Give me an example: a hook-and-loop name tag. First it lies loosely on the jacket, then it grips, then the hooks sink in. *A-then-A-then-I.*"

## Wednesday: the quiz that caught me out

"Quiz Mode on this subtopic. I felt confident. Then: which layer secretes hCG? I picked the cytotrophoblast. Wrong. On the next question I wasn't sure, so I pressed 'I don't know' instead of guessing, and asked why the right answer was right. (Sofia's story about [why practice quizzes can lie](/en/2026/10/practice-quizzes-stop-guessing/) says the same.)

Back in Explain Mode, the Common mistake note was blunt: the syncytiotrophoblast, not the cytotrophoblast, makes hCG. I added my own note to that step."

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

"Give me an example made it stick: a home pregnancy test turns positive because the syncytiotrophoblast releases hCG. The diagram put both layers side by side."

{% include scene.html n=6 %}

## Thursday: my notes in one PDF

"Last section: location matters clinically. Implantation outside the uterine cavity is an ectopic pregnancy, most often in the uterine tube, and the course marks it as a red flag. I highlighted the passage and watched one of the suggested explainers."

"Then the sequence summary and the last mnemonic: *Cut, Compact, Cavitate, Hatch, Hook.* Everything now sits together in one PDF, 'Cleavage, Blastocyst Formation, and Implantation - My Notes': the sections, the diagrams, my note, my highlight, the video links, the examples and the memory aids. I read it twice and slept."

## Friday: the viva

{% include scene.html n=7 %}

"Dr. Haddad leaned back. 'Take me from zygote to implantation.' I didn't rush. Cleavage, morula and compaction, blastocyst, hatching, then apposition, adhesion, invasion. 'And who makes hCG?' The syncytiotrophoblast. He nodded once. 'Clear, and in the right order. That's what I wanted to hear.'"

**Three things Alicia's diary shows:**

1. Go deep on one subtopic instead of skimming the whole course.
2. Press "I don't know" when you're unsure; the gap you admit is the gap you fix.
3. Keep your own notes beside the explanations, in one place you can revise from.
