## Monday 21 September: five weeks of certification exam preparation

Certification exam preparation starts tonight, because this afternoon Desmond closed the door of the glass meeting room and said: "The project manager role opens in January. Pass the certification exam on 24 October and it's yours. Anjali in HR will want more than a certificate, though. Bring a real project plan you wrote yourself."

Five weeks, on top of a full-time job as a project coordinator. I'm keeping this diary so I can tell, honestly, whether I'm getting ready or just getting busy. (Freya, Desmond, Anjali and our freight company are fictional. The gap between busy and ready is not.)

The 600-page guide and the forums would give me volume, not a picture of what I know. So I opened [the LearningClarity study platform](https://www.learningclarity.ai) and typed: "Project management certification, intermediate level: scope, scheduling, cost and earned value, risk, stakeholders and procurement. I run projects day to day; I need the theory and the vocabulary." It built the topic and its subtopics.

## Week two: the streak said yes, my head said no

**Sunday 4 October.** A 13-day streak. Fourteen hours in Explain Mode this week, every section read, Next, Next, Next. Three highlighters dead.

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

Tonight I tried to explain earned value without my notes and got nowhere. The streak counts the days I showed up. It doesn't count what stayed. That's activity, not readiness.

**Monday 5 October.** New rule: test first, reread second. I opened Quiz Mode on four subtopics and promised to press "I don't know" instead of guessing. Result: 58%, with seven "I don't know" answers, five of them on earned value and risk responses. Ugly, but the first honest picture in two weeks. (Sofia learned the same lesson in [the story of practice quizzes that stopped flattering her](/en/2026/10/practice-quizzes-stop-guessing/).)

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

I asked why each right answer was right, went back to earned value, tapped Simplify until it clicked, then Visualize for a labelled diagram that I redrew by hand. I turned risk responses into an AI podcast and let the docked player run on the tram while I browsed my other subtopics. And I invited Desmond as my supervisor by email, with weekly reports. If I'm going to say I'm ready, he should see the trend, not just my word.

## Saturday 17 October: 64% in the library

Library at nine. Exam Mode, set up like the real thing: whole topic, 30 questions, Hard, timed.

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

64%. It stung. The AI performance summary was blunt and useful: strong on scheduling and scope, revisit stakeholder communication and procurement. Earned value, my red circle, came out fine, so the ugly quizzes had worked. There's [research on the testing effect](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26151629/) showing that retrieving what you know is itself practice. That explains a lot about my highlighters.

In the evening I did two short Talk to This calls, explaining my stakeholder plan out loud until I stopped hesitating.

## Wednesday 21 October: the meeting I wasn't in

Desmond and Anjali met today to talk about January. He told me afterwards that they had my weekly report open next to the project plan I'd written for our warehouse move.

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

"The report shows a trend," he said. "Quiz scores climbing, weak areas revisited. But Anjali went through your plan line by line, because no quiz can show whether you can run a kickoff." Fair. The data helps them ask better questions; the judgement is still theirs.

Thursday: a second exam with the same settings. 83%.

## Saturday 31 October: the last tick

The result came yesterday: passed. The exam hall, the evenings and the plan were my own work.

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

Desmond called before I'd finished reading the email. "Anjali says yours is the first plan she didn't send back. January's yours." Tonight I ticked off the last weak area and peeled away the sticky tabs.

## What my diary taught me

- **Activity is not readiness.** Hours and streaks show effort; quizzes and exams show what stuck.
- **"I don't know" is data.** Guessing hides the gap you most need to see.
- **Read the trend, then add a human.** A rising line next to real work convinced more than any single score.
