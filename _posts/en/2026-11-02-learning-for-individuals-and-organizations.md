At 7:10 on a wet Monday evening, Callum read the email twice. Northfold Engineering wanted to interview him for a project coordinator role, and the interview on Friday the 14th would open with a written test on scheduling, risk and stakeholder management. Twelve days.

Across town, Soraya's director stopped at her desk. Five site staff had just been promoted to project coordinators. "Their programme starts in three weeks," he said. "And Marcus still needs a sixth coordinator hired by the end of the month."

Callum, Soraya and Northfold are fictional, but the question is real. This is a story about learning for individuals and organizations: one person studying alone, one team lead training others, and where their paths meet.

## Monday night: Callum's kitchen table

After twenty years as a site supervisor, Callum knew how projects go wrong, but he had never written a risk register or a formal change request. Videos and a 600-page guide would not give him a structure in twelve days.

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

After the children were asleep he opened [the LearningClarity study platform](https://www.learningclarity.ai) and typed: "Project coordination for a site supervisor moving into an office role: scheduling, risk registers, change control and stakeholder updates. I know sites well; I don't know the paperwork."

LearningClarity built the topic and its subtopics. **Explain Mode** went one short section at a time. When the critical path read like a textbook, he tapped **Simplify**. On risk scoring he tapped **Visualize** and redrew the labelled diagram by hand in his notebook.

## Tuesday: Soraya tests her own material first

Soraya's instinct was to email the coordinator handbook to all five and book a room. Instead she exported it from Word to PDF, uploaded it and became a learner herself.

It stung. In **Quiz Mode** on change control she pressed "I don't know" three times, on her own handbook. The section said changes "go to the PM" but never who signs off, or by when. She rewrote two pages, uploaded the new version and took the quiz again.

## Wednesday: the wobble on the 7:12

Callum's first quiz on risk went badly: four "I don't know" answers and a low mastery level. "Twenty years of real risks," he muttered, "and I can't name them on paper."

He asked why each answer was right and used **Explain Further** on risk owners. Every morning on the train he took five questions before reading anything: recall first, then check. Research on [the testing effect](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26151629/) suggests that this kind of retrieval practice strengthens later memory.

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

By the second Thursday the "I don't know" answers were down to one. On Saturday he set **Exam Mode** to timed, Hard, 30 questions. The AI performance summary called his scheduling strong and told him to revisit stakeholder updates.

## Friday the 14th, and the Monday after

Callum left his interview at dusk. On the way out he passed a glass meeting room where a woman in a mustard blouse was briefing five new coordinators. He did not know her name yet.

On Monday Soraya sat down with Marcus from HR. Callum's test score was second of three.

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

"The test is one piece of evidence," Marcus said. "Not the decision." They weighed it with the interview, where Callum had explained how he re-sequenced a job after a flooded basement. (Compare Nevine's [six-day recruitment story](/en/2026/10/mass-recruitment-training-story/).) They offered him the job.

## Week one of the programme

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

Soraya shared the handbook topic by email with each coordinator; everyone who accepted got their own copy and their own progress. Each also accepted her as a supervisor, so a weekly email report showed her, person by person, their recent quizzes and exams.

## Thursday: where the two paths meet

Callum started the following Monday. Soraya shared the topic with him too. On Thursday Ottilie, the youngest coordinator, leaned over his notebook and laughed. "You drew the whole risk matrix by hand?"

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

"Twelve nights at the kitchen table," he said.

His first exam on the handbook flagged one area to revisit: Northfold's own sign-off rules, the part no one can study alone. He showed Soraya the summary. She smiled. "You did on your own what I ask everyone in this room to do. Now you just need our way of doing it."

## Three takeaways

- **Quiz before you trust.** Honest "I don't know" answers beat rereading.
- **Pilot training as a learner.** Take your own material first.
- **A score is one piece of evidence.** Weigh it with the interview and judgement.

Tonight, upload the handbook or notes you already rely on and take a five-question quiz on them yourself, before you study them alone or share them.
