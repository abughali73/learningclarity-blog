At 8:40 on Monday, Rocco's manager forwarded him a one-line email from Brenner Freight's operations director: "Before we renew on Friday, I'll have one question about the new release. I want the answer from Rocco, not from a slide."

Rocco sells route-planning software for Kestrel Routing, and Brenner is his biggest account. The release notes ran to forty pages. Agnes Vogt, the director, was known for asking one question per meeting, and never the easy one. Rocco, Agnes and both companies are fictional, but anyone in sales knows that Monday.

His week was full of client visits, so forty pages at a desk was out. Persistent audio learning would have to do the heavy lifting.

## Monday: the release, in his ears

Ilse, from learning and development, had uploaded the release notes as a PDF to [the LearningClarity study platform](https://www.learningclarity.ai). It built a topic with subtopics: positioning, new features, objections and implementation notes. She shared the topic with the sales team by email.

Rocco accepted, got his own copy and turned it into an **AI podcast**, a two-voice conversation between a host and an expert guest. It was generated in the background, and an "AI job complete" notification told him when it was ready. He pressed play on his way out of the office, coffee in the other hand.

## Tuesday: persistent audio learning on a tram

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

On the dawn tram, the guest moved on to objections, and Rocco wanted to see the subtopic itself. He opened it, and the voices kept going. The episode played on in a small docked player while he scrolled. No restart, no hunting for his place.

Audio he had to babysit on one screen would have been just another lesson. Audio that followed him around fitted into a tram ride.

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

Across town, Ilse was listening back to her own episode, checking it against the release notes. She had read a [2026 systematic review of educational podcasts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42319146/): learners generally valued podcasts as an extra resource, and the authors called the evidence limited. So her note to the team was careful: "Use the episode for the story. The implementation steps are a diagram problem. Don't skip the topic."

Rocco read it on the tram, and skipped the topic anyway.

## Wednesday: a quiz that stung

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

Between visits he replayed the saved episode, mouthing lines about the new features on the pavement. By evening he felt ready.

**Quiz Mode** disagreed. He chose all the subtopics and pressed "I don't know" instead of guessing. The results showed a score, a breakdown of correct, incorrect and "I don't know" answers, and a mastery level. Four of his "I don't know" answers sat in one place, implementation: what happens to existing data during the switch, and in which order. Ilse had been right.

## Thursday: back to the screen

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

On Thursday afternoon he opened the implementation subtopic in **Explain Mode**, one short section at a time. He tapped **Explain Further** for examples, then **Visualize** on the switch-over sequence and got a clean, labelled diagram. He redrew it in his notebook and added Brenner's dispatch team to each step from his own account notes. The diagram explained the steps; fitting them to Brenner was his job.

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

That evening on the sofa he took a second quiz on the implementation subtopics. He asked why two answers were wrong, read the explanations and watched his mastery level rise. One earbud out, he smiled for the first time all week.

## Friday: the question

Brenner's meeting room smelled of coffee and printer toner. Agnes listened to his summary without a word, then leaned forward.

"If we switch on Monday, what happens to the routes my dispatchers already have, and who on my side has to do what that week?"

Rocco opened his notebook to the sketch and walked her through it, step by step, naming her own people at each stage.

Agnes looked at the page for a long moment. "That's the first time a rep has answered that without phoning their technical team." The renewal was signed that afternoon: Rocco's preparation, Rocco's answer.

## Three takeaways

- **Let audio carry the story.** A docked player that keeps playing turns commutes and walks into study time.
- **Keep visual steps on the screen.** Sequences land better as a diagram than as a voice.
- **Check what stayed.** "I don't know" in Quiz Mode shows the gap before a client does.
