On **Sunday 8 November 2026**, the LearningClarity study platform gets new features that rebuild the lesson itself: lessons start while they are still being written, the lesson tools are clearer, every lesson fits your level, and a wrap-up shows what to do next. You can also revise what you missed, play with lesson charts and change your sign-in email. It helps anyone who studies in short sessions or returns to topics over weeks. To show it in use we follow Lina, a fictional first-year student working through a topic called "Photosynthesis basics".

{% include scene.html n=1 %}

## Lessons you can start reading straight away {#f-lessons-start-fast}

Waiting for a whole lesson breaks your focus. Now it appears as it is written: open a subtopic in **Explain Mode** and page 1 is there while the rest is prepared, with an outline of every planned page and a bar that fills as pages are written. Lina opens "Light-dependent reactions" between lectures and starts reading at once.

{% include feature-clip.html id="lessons-start-fast" %}

1. Open a subtopic in Explain Mode and start reading page 1.
2. Leave if you need to: the topic page shows **Writing lesson…** meanwhile.
3. Come back any time. Each lesson is written once and saved: unfinished lessons reopen where you stopped, and finished ones can be reread.

Next morning, Lina lands back on the page where she stopped.

## Redesigned lesson tools {#f-lesson-tools}

Picking a tool should not be a guess. Each tool now says what it works on and what it does before you use it.

{% include feature-clip.html id="lesson-tools" %}

1. Select a sentence, several sentences or a paragraph: the tools appear under it in a box marked **Selected text**. For the whole page, use the box at the end marked **This whole page**.
2. Choose a tool: Simplify rewrites it in simpler words; Explain Further offers options such as Why it works, Worked example, Real-world use, and Limits & mistakes; Visualize offers **Illustration** (an AI-drawn image) or Watch a video; Ask answers your own question and offers Check me and Help me remember.
3. The result appears under the text. Mark it Helpful or Not helpful, add it to your notes, or remove it.

Lina picks Worked example for a paragraph on the electron transport chain and adds it to her notes.

{% include scene.html n=2 %}

## Lessons planned for your level {#f-level-at-creation}

A lesson pitched wrong wastes time or loses you. Now, when you create a topic, an **AI Course** or an **AI Learning Plan**, you choose how familiar you are with it, and every lesson in it is planned and written for that level.

{% include feature-clip.html id="level-at-creation" %}

1. Start creating a topic, course or learning plan.
2. Choose your level, from **New to me** to **I know it well**.
3. Create it. Lina chose **New to me**, so every lesson is written for someone meeting the subject for the first time.

## Retry knowledge checks, then a lesson wrap-up {#f-lesson-wrap-up}

A wrong answer is a chance to try again. **Try another question** now gives you a new one, and you can look back at the earlier questions and your answers. Lina misses a Calvin cycle question and takes another.

{% include feature-clip.html id="lesson-wrap-up" %}

1. Highlight sentences as **Key point** or Confusing, and add notes as you read.
2. At the end, the wrap-up shows how many knowledge checks you got right on the first try, how you did on each part, the page worth another look and your notes.
3. Pick a suggested next step: the next subtopic, a quiz, the exam, the podcast, Talk to This or the PDF. Your notes and answers can be downloaded with the lesson as a PDF, as Lina does that evening.

{% include scene.html n=3 %}

## Revise what you missed {#f-revise}

Seeing mistakes is not fixing them. After a quiz or an exam, each weak area now has a **Revise** button, and **Revise all** works through them one after another. When a quiz shows one weak subtopic, Lina chooses Revise.

{% include feature-clip.html id="revise" %}

1. Review the saved lesson, with the pages for missed questions marked.
2. Answer new questions, one per screen, with instant feedback.
3. See your result: more than half right brings the subtopic back to good standing.

## Play with this chart {#f-play-with-chart}

A fixed chart shows one case. Under every chart in a lesson, **Play with this chart** lets you change it yourself. It is free and uses no AI.

{% include scene.html n=4 %}

1. Find a chart in a lesson.
2. Choose **Play with this chart** underneath it.
3. Change it and watch it update, as Lina does.

## Change your sign-in email {#f-change-email}

When your address changes, your account can follow. Until you confirm the new one, you keep signing in with your current email.

{% include feature-clip.html id="change-email" %}

1. In your profile, choose **Change email**.
2. Type the new address twice and your current password.
3. Open the link sent to the new address within 24 hours. Once you confirm, your other devices are signed out.

If someone else made the change, the message to your old address has a link to secure your account.

{% include scene.html n=5 %}

## Good to know

- All seven features are in the web app only for now; the iPhone and Android apps follow later.
- The lesson level is chosen when you create a topic, course or plan, and cannot be changed later.
- Accounts that sign in with Google, Microsoft or Apple manage their email there.
