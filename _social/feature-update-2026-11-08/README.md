### LearningClarity LinkedIn page

Waiting for a lesson to finish writing is a small thing that breaks focus. As of today, it no longer happens in LearningClarity.

Our 8 November feature update rebuilds the lesson itself in the web app:

• Lessons start straight away: page 1 appears while the rest is written, with an outline of every planned page.
• Redesigned lesson tools: each tool says what it works on and what it does, for selected text or the whole page.
• Lessons planned for your level: choose from "New to me" to "I know it well" when you create a topic, an AI Course or an AI Learning Plan.
• Knowledge checks you can retry, then a wrap-up that suggests your next step.
• Revise what you missed after a quiz or exam, one weak area at a time or all of them in a row.
• Play with this chart: change any chart in a lesson yourself, free and with no AI.
• Change your sign-in email from your profile.

To show it in use, we follow Lina, a fictional first-year student working through "Photosynthesis basics", with short screen clips of every feature.

All seven are in the web app for now; the iPhone and Android apps follow later.

### Maher's personal LinkedIn profile

A lesson pitched at the wrong level wastes a learner's time or loses them. This week we changed when that decision is made.

In LearningClarity, when you create a topic, a course or a learning plan, you now say how familiar you are with it, from "New to me" to "I know it well", and every lesson in it is planned and written for that level from the start.

It is one of seven changes in today's feature update, and most of them are about the lesson itself. Lessons now start while they are still being written, so page 1 is there to read while the rest is prepared, not after the whole lesson is done. Each lesson tool says what it works on and what it does before you use it. A wrong answer in a knowledge check leads to another question instead of a dead end, and a wrap-up at the end suggests what to do next. After a quiz or exam, Revise takes you straight back to what you missed.

The post follows Lina, a fictional first-year student, through one topic, with short screen clips of each feature. Everything is in the web app first; the phone apps follow later.
