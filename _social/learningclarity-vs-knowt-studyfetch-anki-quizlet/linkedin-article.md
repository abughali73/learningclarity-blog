## The myth: more flashcards, more ready

Beatriz believed the answer was cards. One classmate drilled shared Quizlet sets, another had built thousands of Anki cards, a third asked StudyFetch's AI tutor about everything, and a fourth used Knowt for its generous free plan.

But writing cards would eat a week she didn't have, and cards would not show whether she understood the material. She needed to learn the material in order, rehearse the real exam and study away from her desk.

## Week one: one subject, one path

She uploaded the PDF to [the LearningClarity study platform](https://www.learningclarity.ai) and exported the slide decks to PDF first, since Word and PowerPoint files can't be uploaded directly. LearningClarity built a topic and its subtopics from the content, and she chose the ones on her syllabus.

Each evening she took one subtopic in **Explain Mode**, one short section at a time. When contract clauses blurred together she tapped **Simplify**; when a supplier-evaluation model felt abstract, **Visualize** drew a labelled diagram. Knowledge Checks showed whether each section had landed.

## The commute becomes study time

On the tram she played a two-voice AI podcast of the subtopic she had studied the night before. When something still felt unclear, she opened **Talk to This** and asked the live voice tutor out loud, in short calls between stops.

![Beatriz on a morning tram with earbuds in, listening closely to a study podcast](https://blog.learningclarity.ai/assets/img/posts/learningclarity-vs-knowt-studyfetch-anki-quizlet-scene-3.jpg)

## Week four: the practice exam that stung

Her first timed practice exam in **Exam Mode**, on the whole topic at Hard difficulty, came back lower than she had hoped. The performance summary was clear: strong on sourcing strategy, weak on contract risk.

It stung, but it helped. Instead of rereading 300 pages, she returned to the contract-risk subtopics, used **Quiz Mode** with "I don't know" instead of guessing, and asked why each wrong answer was wrong. Later practice exams showed the gap closing.

## The best study app for exams depends on the job

Each tool does a different job. Prices were checked in November 2026 and change often.

- **LearningClarity**: Approach: Explain, Quiz, Exam; What you bring: PDFs, images, text, or a described topic; Testing: Quizzes and timed exams; Audio and voice: Podcasts, Listen, Talk to This; Best for: Understanding and exam rehearsal.
- **Knowt**: Approach: Notes and flashcards; What you bring: Your notes, Quizlet imports; Testing: Learn mode, spaced repetition; Audio and voice: Not the focus; Best for: Free flashcard study.
- **StudyFetch**: Approach: AI tutor on your files; What you bring: Uploads; lectures with Premium; Testing: Tutor questions; Audio and voice: Live lecture assistant (Premium); Best for: Questions about dense material.
- **Anki**: Approach: Spaced repetition; What you bring: Cards you write or import; Testing: Card reviews; Audio and voice: Not the focus; Best for: Long-term memorisation.
- **Quizlet**: Approach: Flashcard sets; What you bring: Your sets or ready-made sets; Testing: Learn and Test rounds; Audio and voice: Not the focus; Best for: Courses with existing sets.

- **Choose LearningClarity** to learn your own material in order, rehearse timed exams and study by audio. It has no community deck library, takes no audio or video sources, and is not a long-term spaced-repetition scheduler.
- **Choose Knowt** for free flashcards: unlimited cards and notes, Learn and spaced repetition, one-click Quizlet import; AI features are capped monthly and the free tier shows ads ([MakerStack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/knowt-review/)).
- **Choose StudyFetch** if you learn by asking: its Spark.E tutor answers from your uploads. The free plan gives 10 tutor chats, 1 study set and 2 uploads; Premium ($11.99 a month) adds a live lecture assistant ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/study-fetch)).
- **Choose Anki** to keep thousands of facts for months. Its FSRS scheduler needs fewer reviews than the older default for the same retention, but you build or import every card ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm)).
- **Choose Quizlet** if a set for your course already exists. Free Learn and Test rounds are capped; Plus costs $35.99 a year (20 Learn rounds and 3 practice tests a month), Plus Unlimited $44.99 a year ([Mindomax](https://www.mindomax.com/quizlet-plus-cost)).

Many learners combine tools: LearningClarity to understand and rehearse, Anki to keep core facts fresh afterwards. For another comparison, read [how LearningClarity fits a daily study routine next to NotebookLM](https://blog.learningclarity.ai/en/2026/10/ai-study-routine-notebooklm/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is LearningClarity a flashcard app?**

No. LearningClarity teaches a topic in Explain Mode and then tests it with Quiz Mode and Exam Mode. It has no community deck library and is not a long-term spaced-repetition scheduler.

**Can I use LearningClarity together with Anki?**

Yes. Many learners use LearningClarity to understand and rehearse a subject, and Anki to keep a core set of facts fresh for months afterwards.

**Which files can I upload to LearningClarity?**

PDFs, images (PNG, JPG or JPEG) and plain text files. Export Word documents and PowerPoint decks to PDF first; audio, video and YouTube links are not supported as sources.
